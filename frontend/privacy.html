Data & Privacy

KNOW operates in Kenya, so the law that governs everything here is the Data Protection Act, 2019 (DPA), enforced by the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC). It applies to any organisation handling personal data of people in Kenya — there's no small-business exemption from the Act itself, only from certain registration paperwork (below).

Who's the "data controller" here?

This matters because the DPA puts different duties on different roles:

  • Each institution (a school, shop, or supermarket using KNOW) is its own data controller for the people it deals with day to day — students' parents, customers, suppliers. It decides why that data is collected.
  • KNOW itself acts as a data processor for that institution data — it stores and moves it, but doesn't decide why it's collected.
  • KNOW is the data controller for one thing directly: the staff/admin accounts used to log in (usernames, names, roles, activity logs) — that's data the platform itself decides how to use.

In practice: an institution onboarding onto KNOW should know it likely has its own registration and privacy-notice obligations for its own customers/parents — separate from KNOW's obligations for the account/login data.

Do institutions need to register with the ODPC?

Under the Data Protection (Registration of Data Controllers and Data Processors) Regulations, 2021, registration is required unless an entity has both:

  • Annual turnover/revenue under KES 5,000,000, and
  • Fewer than 10 employees

Schools are on the mandatory-registration list regardless of size — so any school institution onboarded here should register with the ODPC even if it's tiny. Shops and supermarkets fall under the general turnover/headcount exemption unless they separately trigger a mandatory category (e.g. running CCTV, which many retail premises do).

Registration is done directly with the ODPC (odpc.go.ke), not through this platform — it's the institution's own responsibility, not KNOW's.

The core rules, in plain terms

PrincipleWhat it means here
Lawfulness & transparencyOnly collect data with a real reason, and don't hide what you're doing with it.
Purpose limitationData collected for payroll shouldn't quietly get reused for marketing, etc.
Data minimisationIf a field isn't actually needed, don't add it "just in case."
AccuracyWrong data (a misspelled name, stale phone number) should be correctable.
Storage limitationDon't keep data forever — delete or archive it once it's served its purpose.
SecurityPasswords hashed, connections encrypted, access limited to who actually needs it.
AccountabilityBe able to show what you collect and why, if the ODPC ever asks.

None of this falls into the DPA's narrower "sensitive personal data" category (health, biometric, genetic, ethnic origin, religion, sexual orientation, etc.) — that's intentional. Keeping it that way is the single easiest way to stay low-risk.

People's rights — what's built, what's not yet

  • Built Right to rectification — accounts can edit their own name/username, and admins can correct staff records.
  • Built Right to access their own account — /api/users/me and the Settings page.
  • Partial Right to erasure — accounts can be deactivated, but there's no full "delete my data" flow yet. Worth building if the platform grows.
  • Partial Data portability — no self-service export of one's own data yet.

Action checklist for you as super admin

  1. Confirm which onboarded institutions are schools (mandatory ODPC registration) and flag it to them directly — most won't know.
  2. For shop/supermarket institutions, check whether they run CCTV or otherwise fall into a mandatory-registration category before assuming the small-business exemption applies.
  3. Publish a short privacy notice institutions can point their own customers/parents to, covering the M-Pesa payer-name/code capture.
  4. Periodically review the activity log's size and consider archiving entries older than a set period (e.g. 2 years) once volume grows.
  5. Avoid adding any new fields to account or transaction forms without checking they're actually necessary — this page is a good place to start that conversation.
